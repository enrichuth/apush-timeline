Major Turning Points — Chronological

1619
First Enslaved Africans Arrive; Virginia House of Burgesses
Two pivotal events in one year: beginnings of chattel slavery AND representative government in America.
Colonial · Labor · Government
1676
Bacon's Rebellion
Class conflict in Virginia reveals tension between backcountry settlers and elites — leads planters to shift from indentured servants to African slaves.
Colonial · Race · Labor
1776
Declaration of Independence
Enlightenment principles applied to justify revolution. "All men are created equal" — yet slavery continues. Sets standard for future reform movements.
Revolution · Ideology
1820
Missouri Compromise
Slavery admitted in Missouri, banned north of 36°30' line. First major compromise — masks rather than resolves sectional conflict.
Sectionalism · Slavery
1848
Seneca Falls Convention
First women's rights convention; Declaration of Sentiments modeled on the Declaration of Independence. Beginning of organized women's suffrage movement.
Reform · Gender
1865
13th Amendment — Abolition of Slavery
Formally ends slavery in the United States. But Black Codes immediately emerge to restrict African American freedom.
Civil War · Reconstruction
1896
Plessy v. Ferguson
"Separate but equal" codifies legal segregation. Sets the framework for Jim Crow — overturned by Brown v. Board in 1954.
SCOTUS · Civil Rights
1920
19th Amendment — Women's Suffrage
After 72 years of organized activism, women gain the right to vote. Note: Black women still largely disenfranchised in the South.
Progressive Era · Gender
1933
New Deal Begins (FDR)
Response to Great Depression. Expands federal government's role. Programs include FDIC, Social Security, Wagner Act. Shifts Democratic coalition.
Economy · Federal Power
1954
Brown v. Board of Education
SCOTUS unanimously overturns Plessy. Segregation in public schools is unconstitutional. Catalyzes the modern Civil Rights Movement.
SCOTUS · Civil Rights
1964–65
Civil Rights Act + Voting Rights Act
Most significant civil rights legislation since Reconstruction. Bans discrimination in public places, employment. Voting Rights Act eliminates literacy tests and poll taxes.
Civil Rights · LBJ Great Society
1973
Watergate + Vietnam War End
Nixon resigns amid Watergate scandal. Vietnam War ends with U.S. withdrawal. Trust in government collapses — long-term political cynicism.
Cold War · Politics
1980
Reagan Revolution
Conservative realignment: supply-side economics, deregulation, anti-communism abroad. Reshapes both political parties for decades.
Conservatism · Economy
2001
September 11 Attacks
Al-Qaeda attacks reshape American foreign policy, civil liberties debate (Patriot Act), and lead to the longest wars in U.S. history.
Foreign Policy · Civil Liberties
02

Key Supreme Court Cases

Case & Year Issue Ruling Significance
Marbury v. Madison 1803 Can the Supreme Court declare laws unconstitutional? Yes — Judicial Review Establishes SCOTUS power to strike down laws. Most important case in US history.
McCulloch v. Maryland 1819 Can Congress create a national bank? Can states tax it? Broad federal power; states cannot tax federal Establishes "necessary and proper" (elastic) clause as broad federal authority.
Dred Scott v. Sandford 1857 Are enslaved people citizens with constitutional rights? No citizenship; Missouri Compromise unconstitutional Inflames sectional crisis. Later overturned by 14th Amendment.
Plessy v. Ferguson 1896 Is racial segregation constitutional? Yes — "Separate but equal" Validates Jim Crow for 58 years. Overturned by Brown v. Board 1954.
Schenck v. United States 1919 Does the 1st Amendment protect speech that endangers national security? No — "Clear and Present Danger" test Limits free speech during wartime. Sets precedent for later cases.
Korematsu v. United States 1944 Was Japanese American internment constitutional? Yes (6-3) Widely condemned as a civil liberties failure. Formally repudiated in 2018 by Trump v. Hawaii.
Brown v. Board of Education 1954 Is racial segregation in public schools constitutional? No — overturns Plessy Landmark civil rights ruling. Sparks "massive resistance" in the South.
Engel v. Vitale 1962 Can public schools require prayer? No — violates Establishment Clause Warren Court's expansive reading of separation of church and state.
Gideon v. Wainwright 1963 Must states provide attorneys to defendants who can't afford one? Yes Incorporates 6th Amendment right to counsel against states.
Roe v. Wade 1973 Does the Constitution protect a woman's right to abortion? Yes — privacy right (trimester framework) Overturned by Dobbs v. Jackson 2022. Central to culture wars for 50 years.
United States v. Nixon 1974 Can the President claim executive privilege to withhold evidence? No — must comply with subpoena Unanimously rules against Nixon. Triggers his resignation. Rule of law over executive.
